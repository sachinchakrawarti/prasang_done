Pothī paḍhi paḍhi jaga muā, paṇḍita bhayā na koya | Ḍhāī ākhara prema kā, paḍhe so paṇḍita hoya ||
IAST Standard Sadhukkadi Dialect

"Reading books over and over, the whole world died, yet none became truly wise.
One who reads just two and a half letters of Love becomes the true scholar."

Translator's Note

"Two and a half letters" refers to the Hindi word प्रेम (prem) — often said to be composed of 2.5 aksharas in the poetic tradition. Kabir contrasts intellectual book-learning with the simple, transformative power of love.

Metaphor Decoded

"Two and a half letters of love" (ḍhāī ākhara prem kā) is a playful reference to the word prem — Kabir suggests that infinite wisdom can be compressed into a single, simple syllable of devotion.

Historical Context

Composed during the Bhakti movement, when vernacular poets challenged the Sanskrit monopoly of religious knowledge. Kabir critiques both Brahminical book-learning and Islamic ritualism.

Poetic Device

Antithesis — the first line presents the futility of endless scholarship (paḍhi paḍhi), while the second line offers the antidote: a single act of love.

Similar Poems

Compare with Rumi's "reed pipe" metaphor — both poets contrast scholarly pride with the raw longing of the soul.
